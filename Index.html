Fashion Site Builder

Your daily learning program

Recommended Webflow
Best for editorial, art-directed fashion brands. You control every pixel, animation, and scroll interaction. The early All Saints website felt like a dark room you walked into — Webflow can do that. Steeper learning curve than Shopify, but you will actually learn how websites work.
Best for: lookbook sites, storytelling, custom interactions, under ~50 SKUs
Alternative Shopify
Better when you need robust checkout, inventory, and shipping out of the box. The design is more templated, but you can customize themes with Liquid code. Easier to scale when you have many products or variants.
Best for: higher SKU counts, complex shipping, rapid scaling
Recommendation: Start with Webflow + Webflow Ecommerce. For a premium, slow-fashion brand with few pieces, the editorial control matters more than backend features. You can always migrate to Shopify later if you scale past ~30 products.

Photo Strategy
Best Commission a shoot
For a premium brand at All Saints price points, original photography is non-negotiable. Stock photos destroy trust at this level.
Budget option
Find a local fashion photography student or emerging photographer. Trade clothes for images. You need: 1 hero look, 3–4 detail shots, 1 atmospheric environment shot.
Mid option
Hire a photographer + model for a half day. Focus on natural light, minimal retouching. All Saints early imagery was often shot in raw warehouses or against distressed walls.
DIY starter
If budget is truly zero, shoot flat lays on a dark concrete or raw wood surface using natural window light and a smartphone. Keep it consistent.
Temporary Curated stock
If you must use stock while waiting for a shoot, use it only for atmospheric/background imagery — never for product.
• Unsplash (search: "raw portrait," "textured fabric," "industrial interior")
• Pexels (filter by dark/moody)
• Stocksy (paid, but editorial and authentic feeling)
Rule: if a customer could find the same image on another brand's site, do not use it.

All Saints Vibe Guide
Use this as a shared reference between you and your designer.
Visual language
Color palette
Near-black backgrounds (#0a0a0a, #111), warm off-white text (#e8e4df), muted metallics (brass, gunmetal) as accents. Avoid pure white and pure black.
Imagery
Low-key lighting, visible texture, slight grain. Models look like they have somewhere to be, not like they are posing. Environments feel found, not designed.
Typography
Large, confident serif headlines (Tiempos, Canela, Lyon). Small, functional sans-serif for navigation and product details. Generous line-height on body.
Layout
Asymmetric grids, full-bleed images, lots of negative space. Information is revealed slowly — no clutter, no urgency.
Motion
Slow, heavy transitions. Page loads feel like a curtain rising. Hover states are subtle — a slight darkening, a slow zoom, never a bounce.
What to avoid
Bright, saturated colors
Playful animations (bounces, elastic snaps)
Rounded, friendly UI (big pill buttons, soft shadows)
Overly clean / sterile product photography
Sale banners, countdown timers, urgency messaging
Social proof carousels ("What our customers say")
